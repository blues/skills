# Source handling and useful requests

Explain the actual workflow before requesting restricted material: read the supplied
sources to understand product behavior, draft reusable meanings rather than source text,
review consequential statements and sharing restrictions, and publish only within the
agreed scope. Do not promise that reading a file “in place” keeps its contents out of the
model service or transcript. Storage, retention and processing depend on the harness;
state what is known and consult its actual controls for anything unknown. A confidentiality
question deserves a direct answer about this workflow, not an invented guarantee.

Supplying source for training authorizes useful drafting of its field meanings, units,
sentinels, encodings, formulas and cadence. It does not automatically authorize sharing
all derived details with every project reader. Paraphrased calibration constants,
contractual thresholds and operating practices can still be sensitive. Separate such a
detail for review when a restriction applies; continue the unaffected work. Keep private
working drafts in harness-owned storage, outside source repositories supplied for reading.
Describe their actual lifecycle and offer an export or durable handoff where appropriate.
Explain that the person can inspect, correct or remove draft wording before publication.
Read back the consequential meanings during each lesson, including what an answer based
on them would claim.

If asked about confidentiality, answer the two parts separately: what this training
will put into shared skills, and what the current harness/account does with supplied
material. State the known behavior directly; a general policy page does not establish
this account's retention settings. Offer the smallest relevant artifact or an interview
about the meanings it would establish if the person cannot share it.

## Ask for the artifact that answers the gap

- **Host firmware:** request the relevant Notefile templates, `note.add` producers,
  environment reads and state transitions, with a version. These reveal wire types,
  units, encoding, sentinels, conditional emission and derived formulas. Comments can
  explain intent; verify behavior in code and deployment scope. Do not imply firmware
  is the only possible source of every formula.
- **Requirements/design:** connect thresholds, audiences, consequences and product
  intent that telemetry cannot explain.
- **Manuals, datasheets, support articles and dashboards:** recover customer vocabulary,
  operating procedures, expected units and the answer shapes people already use.

Say what the specific artifact would settle and what interview work it saves. Ask whether
it is public or restricted. Accept “not now”; record a promised source by role and date
in the question queue only when still needed. Ask again when it matters, not automatically
on every session. When it arrives, explain the uncertainties it resolved. Never request
credentials or route secrets as product evidence.

Make the request concrete: “The template and `note.add` producer for this Notefile would
settle its encoding, sentinels and cadence, saving you those field-by-field questions.”
Name an unresolved meaning when it becomes relevant rather than silently guessing around
the missing source. At a session pause, identify promised sources that are still needed.

## Scope material to the data it explains

A newer or unreleased firmware version can help explain a product without establishing
how deployed devices behave. Ask which version produced the records and which derived
details may be shared. Keep confirmed differences at their owning fields with version,
cohort and time scope; mark not-yet-observed behavior explicitly. Do not merge future and
current meanings or publish restricted plans merely because the source was supplied.
An unresolved version conflict belongs in the evidence and question worklist.

## What belongs in shared knowledge

Write the behavior in your own words, sufficient for a future agent to act without
reading the source. Do not copy private source code, comments, paths, internal symbols,
transcripts or raw data dumps into project skills. Use source IDs with role/version/date
and applicability; approved public documents may have useful titles and citations.

Preserve the on-wire Notefile and field names, API-visible environment-variable keys,
and product/variant names needed to decode and discuss the data. They are interface and
product vocabulary even when source code also uses them as symbols. Removing private
implementation detail must not erase the decoder that makes the skills useful.

Prefer role descriptions and reusable population rules over personal names and deployment
identifiers. Keep runtime project/device IDs, account details, private interests, monitor
recipients, connection readiness and cache state in the agent's private context. If an
identifier is essential to an explicitly authorized deployment-specific rule, label its
scope and refresh owner rather than pretending it is portable product knowledge. Never
publish a credential. Remove unnecessary identifying details without erasing the rule.

Before publication, review against the actual readership and access settings, including
parent/subproject inheritance and any public-read configuration. Don't promise “viewer
only” confidentiality. Distinguish a documented public fact from a restricted fact whose
source merely happens to be paraphrased. Meaning review and permission to share are
separate questions; honor permissions already supplied.
